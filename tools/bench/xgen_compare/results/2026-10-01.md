## What the numbers say

- **Creation**: XGen's `xgmCreateSplineDescription` takes ~11-12 s on this 17k-triangle scalp
  at either count (setup that scales with the mesh, not the strands); GroomLab is ready in
  0.2 s (100k) / 0.45 s (500k), 0.3 / 0.9 s to the first drawn frame.
- **Evaluation** (batch, no drawing): tweaks are close. 100k: clump 51 vs 41 ms (31 without
  Curl), noise 32 vs 36 ms (25). 500k: clump 248 vs 188 ms (140), noise 160 vs 164 ms (113).
  Deforming scalp, per frame: 128 vs 75 ms (100k), 414 vs 325 ms (500k); GroomLab followScalp
  13 / 43 ms.
- **Viewport**: a tweak to the redrawn frame is where XGen wins: noise 31 vs 138 ms (100k), 132
  vs 563 ms (500k). GroomLab's evaluation is under a third of that; the rest is rebuilding and
  uploading its viewport buffers (see below). Playback on the deforming scalp is about even
  without followScalp (5.2 vs 5.7 fps at 100k, 1.5 vs 1.4 fps at 500k) and GroomLab is ahead
  with it (8.4 / 2.1 fps), bound by the redraw rather than the evaluation. Orbit: XGen 557 vs
  316 fps at 100k (GroomLab varies 249-557 between runs), 124 vs 242 fps at 500k.
- **Memory**: process working set after creation (batch) 286 vs 138 MB (100k), 1127 vs 600 MB
  (500k). With the viewport, GroomLab's process holds more (2.1 vs 1.6 GB at 500k) and XGen's
  GPU use is higher (958 vs 486 MB on the device).
- **Files**: XGen stores the splines in the scene (.mb 24 MB at 100k, 114 MB at 500k); GroomLab's
  scene holds its guides and graph (2.2 MB at any count). Neither wrote side files.
- **Exports**: Alembic, 24 frames: 4.7 vs 2.1 s (100k), 18.1 vs 10.5 s (500k); GroomLab with
  followScalp 0.7 / 4.0 s. `.ass` export at 500k: 2.6 vs 0.7 s static, 8.7 vs 2.5 s with motion
  blur.
- **Renders**: XGen is faster at 500k (11.2 vs 21.4 s static, 15.3 vs 31.2 s motion blur) and
  close at 100k (9.7 vs 10.7 s). Without Curl GroomLab renders in 12.7 s at 500k and 7.9 s at
  100k (ahead of XGen there): most of the gap is the curled, tighter-clumped geometry (caveats).
- **Brush**: GroomLab's comb dab (on ~500 guides, strands re-evaluated and redrawn) is 223 ms at
  100k and 738 ms at 500k. XGen's brushes cannot be driven by script (`xgmBrushManip -stroke`
  ran without moving any spline), so there is no XGen number.

## A GroomLab performance finding (not fixed here)

After an evaluation, GroomLab's viewport costs more than the evaluation itself at these counts:
tweak-to-redrawn-frame minus tweak-to-evaluated is ~60-100 ms at 100k and ~390 ms at 500k
(followScalp playback at 500k: 43 ms of evaluation per frame, ~470 ms per drawn frame). The lit
hair item carries positions plus a normal, tangent, bitangent and uv per point (~56 bytes per
point, ~0.45 GB at 500k x 16 points: `resizeFrames`/`writeFrame` in core/src/display.cpp), and
every evaluation rebuilds and uploads all of it. XGen's tweak-to-redraw is close to its
evaluation time. Worth profiling: zero-filling `resize` of fresh vectors, the upload in the draw
override, a lighter vertex format, uploading only what changed. (Since fixed: noise tweak to the
redrawn frame 193 ms at 500k, 44 ms at 100k; see [2026-10-01_viewport.md](2026-10-01_viewport.md).)

## Caveats

- Different systems doing different work: IGS evaluates every spline through its modifiers;
  GroomLab scatters, interpolates ~500 guides and runs its graph. IGS 2022 has no per-strand curl
  or coil, and the clump's `curl` had no effect here, so XGen does no curl; "GroomLab without
  Curl" is the matched operator set. Clump and noise algorithms and units differ; GroomLab's
  clumps are much tighter at the same 0.8 (see the renders).
- "Evaluated" is a forced pull of each system's output (XGen `dgeval` of the description's
  `outSplineData`, GroomLab `getAttr outStrandCount`); GUI numbers include drawing.
- XGen uses OpenCL in the GUI (not in mayapy); GroomLab evaluates on the CPU. Working sets do not
  include GPU memory (the nvidia-smi row does, for the whole device).
- MtoA writes IGS as an `xgenProcedural` whose modifiers run inside kick; GroomLab writes
  evaluated curves: compare export + render, not either alone. IGS curves are b-splines with 18
  points and a constant radius, GroomLab's linear with 16 points and tapered. Renders are
  watermarked (unlicensed); the timings count.
- XGen makes 489k splines when asked for 500k (2% fewer).
- One machine, one session; it varies up to +-30% between days. Systems alternated order every
  run; compare within this file.
