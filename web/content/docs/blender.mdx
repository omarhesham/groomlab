GroomLab for Blender provides the same grooming workflow as the Maya plug-in: the same **Groom
Tools** (Place, Draw, Comb, Smooth, Cut, Paint, Select, Freeze...), guide groups and painted maps,
a **pie menu** on **Y** in place of Maya's marking menu, and GroomLab's Graph Editor with the same
node categories and presets as Maya and the GroomLab app. A groom saved from Blender evaluates to the same strands in
Maya, and the other way round.

It is a Blender **extension** for Blender 5.2 LTS on Windows (x64), distributed from our site.

## Install

<Steps>
<Step>

### Download the extension

[Download GroomLab for Blender](https://downloads.getgroomlab.com/blender/GroomLab-Blender-windows-x64-latest.zip).
Do not unzip it; Blender installs the zip directly.

</Step>
<Step>

### Install from Disk

In Blender choose **Edit > Preferences > Get Extensions**, open the **⌄** menu at the top right and
choose **Install from Disk...**, then pick the zip. GroomLab appears in the list, enabled.

</Step>
<Step>

### Open the GroomLab tab

In the 3D viewport press **N** for the sidebar and pick the **GroomLab** tab.

</Step>
</Steps>

<Callout type="info" title="Updates through Blender">
  Add `https://downloads.getgroomlab.com/blender/index.json` as a remote repository (**Get
  Extensions > Repositories > + > Add Remote Repository**) and Blender offers new GroomLab versions
  with its own updates. Installing a newer zip from disk works too.
</Callout>

## Make a groom

1. Select the mesh the hair grows on (the scalp or the whole head, at real size: a head is about
   0.2 m), with a UV map.
2. Choose **GroomLab > Create Groom from Selection** in the top bar (or **Object > Create Groom
   from Selection**, or the same button in the Groom Tools). Pick a **preset** to start from; **Starter Guides**
   spreads some guides over the scalp, or leave it at 0 and place them yourself, as in Maya.

GroomLab adds a Curves object **`<mesh>_hair`** parented to the mesh. It holds the evaluated
strands, is not selectable, and is rewritten whenever the groom changes. The guides are not
Blender curves: they are GroomLab's guide hierarchy (as in Maya), drawn as an overlay and edited
with the Groom Tools.

## Groom Tools

The **GroomLab** tab of the 3D viewport's sidebar (**N**) opens on **Groom Tools**, the equivalent
of Maya's Groom Tools panel:

- At the top: the **groom** the tools work on, its eye (show or hide the strands), the **Graph
  Editor** and a **More** menu.
- **Your first groom** walks you through placing guides and giving the groom a look.
- **Group**: the guide group the brushes edit and new guides go into (**All groups**: every
  guide); **Isolate** dims the other levels.
- **Tools** and **Paint** tabs. The tools come by category, with Maya's icons:
  **Create** (Place, Draw, Part, Flow), **Shape** (Comb, Slide, Smooth, Straighten, Lift, Twist,
  Curl, Attract, Direction, Noise, Tube), **Trim** (Cut, Length, Erase), **Paint** (Paint) and
  **Guides** (Select, Freeze, and Unfreeze All beside them). Shift+click a guide with
  a brush to solo it (the rest freeze); Shift+click off a guide to unfreeze them all.
- The active tool's **card** shows what it does and exactly its settings, by Maya's names:
  **Radius** (pixels), **Strength** (±: tools that work both ways go negative), **Falloff**,
  **Falloff curve**, **Root to tip**, **Symmetry** (World X or Topology), **Selected only**, **Pen
  pressure**, **Keep off scalp**, **Front facing only**; Place and Draw's **New guides** (Match
  nearby guides, Solo new guides, Length, Segments, Spacing), Paint's map, Comb's
  **Stretch**, what shaping brushes act on (Guides, Parts, Flow), what Erase erases, the next
  part's name, Flow's arrows or sinks (Whorl, Gather, Part out), the Select tool's lasso or box
  and soft radius, the freeze mask. Each tool keeps its own strength.
- **Undo** / **Redo** name the groom's last edit.
- Folded below: **While sculpting** (guides only while a stroke is live; full density), **Guide
  groups & edits** (new group, rename, select, show/hide, delete; **Edit Guides**: Children, Sync
  ±X, Mirror Group, Lock, Freeze, Delete Selected, Promote/Demote...) and **Display**.

Pick a tool and drag over the groom in the viewport. The brush ring follows the cursor on the
scalp in Maya's colors (yellow; red for Part and Erase, orange for Flow, green for Place and Draw)
and highlights the guides it would affect. During a stroke the hair is drawn at a reduced density;
the full preview density returns on release.

### Keys while a tool is active

| Keys | |
|---|---|
| **Ctrl** + stroke | The opposite (Ctrl+Length shrinks, Ctrl+Lift lays down, Ctrl+Freeze unfreezes, Ctrl+Paint on the density map removes hair) |
| **Shift** + drag | Smooth instead |
| **Shift** + click | Solo the guide (the rest freeze) and brush its group; off a guide, unfreeze them all |
| **B** + drag, **M** + drag | The brush radius, the strength (Maya's hotkeys) |
| **S** | The Select tool (drag a lasso; Shift toggles, Ctrl deselects; click a guide) |
| **Page Up** / **Page Down** | The group a level up or down |
| **Y** | The pie menu |
| **Ctrl+Z** / **Ctrl+Shift+Z** | Undo / redo the groom's edits (strokes, guide edits, maps) |
| **Esc** or right-click | Cancel a live stroke; **Esc** again leaves the tool |

Navigation (middle mouse, wheel, numpad) works as always while a tool is active. Outside a
GroomLab tool, Ctrl+Z is Blender's own undo.

### The pie menu

**Y** in the viewport opens GroomLab's pie menu, laid out like Maya's marking menu: **Comb** at the top, **Slide**,
**Smooth**, **Cut**, **Place** at the bottom, **Draw**, **Length**, **Lift**, and below it the rest
of the tools, Select, Freeze and Groom Tools. Change its key in **Edit > Preferences > Add-ons >
GroomLab**.

## Paint maps

The **Paint** tab lists the groom's maps: 0-1 values (or colors) painted on the scalp that any
param can read through a Map node. **New Map** makes one on the guides, on the scalp's vertices,
or as a texture. Pick it, take the **Paint** tool and paint toward the **Value** (a color map: the
**Color**, in the channels ticked); Ctrl paints toward 0 (a color map: toward the inverse),
Shift+drag smooths. **Fill** sets a guide map everywhere (or on the selected guides). **Show on**
colors the guides by a guide map, or shows the map on the scalp while painting.

To paint a setting, set **New Map**'s **For** to **Density**, **Width**, **Length**, **Clump
strength**, **Curl**, **Noise amplitude**, **Flyaways** (the ones the graph has a node for), or
**Other setting...** for any mappable param, pick vertices or a texture, and click OK. A white map
is created, connected to the setting, selected and painted with the **Paint** tool, in one undo
step; if the setting already reads a map, that map is picked instead. Strokes paint toward the
**Value** (1: the setting's full value); **Ctrl** paints toward 0, so Ctrl on the density map
removes hair.

## Viewport display

Guides are drawn as in Maya: colored by level (or by a painted layer, or a hue per group), frozen
guides blue, the selection white with its soft-selection falloff orange, root and tip dots, tubes
when shown, part lines red, flow arrows orange. **Display** (under Groom Tools) has Maya's toggles: Guides,
Tubes, Flow, Strands, the guide points, the guide colors and the **Strand Colors** (Solid,
Color set, Attribute, Random, Clump, Density). Strand colors are a color attribute on the hair
(`groomlab_display`): **Show in Viewport** sets Solid shading's color to Attribute. Renders keep
the hair's material.

## Presets and the graph

**Presets** lists the shipped looks and your own (shared with Maya and the app) by category.
**Apply** replaces the groom's graph with the preset's, fitted to the size of your scalp (your
guides stay). **Insert After Node** adds a preset's look (frizz, flyaways, gravity...) after the
selected node, as one compound node.

**Graph** lists the groom's nodes, the strands' chain from the roots to the output first. Select a
node to edit its params below the list:

- Params come in groups (click a group's name to open it) with the rarely needed ones folded into
  **Advanced**; params that don't apply to the node's current mode are hidden.
- Lengths and widths show in the scene's units (Blender's own unit display), densities in hairs
  per cm², per-length rates per scene unit, fractions as sliders. GroomLab stores centimeters, as
  Maya does, so a groom means the same everywhere.
- Ramps and painted maps are shown here; edit ramps in the Graph Editor (below), paint maps in
  Maya or the GroomLab app.

**+** adds a node of the **New Node** type after the selected one, **−** removes the selected node
(the chain closes over it) and the arrows move it along the chain. For the whole graph, open the
Graph Editor.

## The Graph Editor

**Graph Editor** (in the GroomLab tab's Graph panel) opens the groom's graph in a node editor of
its own, as Maya's GroomLab Graph Editor shows it. Any node editor shows it too: set its type to
**GroomLab Graph** and pick the groom's graph (`<mesh> Graph`). Choosing another groom in the
editor's header (or the GroomLab tab) shows that groom's graph.

- **Nodes** carry their type's ports, colored by what flows through them as in Maya: strands
  orange, guides blue, meshes green, fields violet, curves yellow, strands-or-guides grey. A
  param exposed as an input is a diamond port. Each node is tinted with its category's color
  (Grow, Shape, Look, Color...) and shows a couple of the params you changed.
- **Add** a node with **Shift+A** or **Tab** (search), by the same categories as Maya and the app
  (Sources, Grow, Shape, Deform, Collide, Look...), each node's description as its tooltip. Drag from a
  port to wire it; a wire the graph can't take (a mesh into strands, a loop) is refused and the
  sidebar says why. **X** or **Delete** removes nodes, **Shift+D** or **Ctrl+D** duplicates them
  with their params, **F2** renames, **M** or **B** bypasses a node (its input passes straight
  through).
- **V** (Focus) shows the groom up to the selected node in the viewport instead of the output's;
  renders keep the output. On a Clump it shows its clump centers as dots on the scalp too. **Set as Output** makes a node the groom's output. **F** frames the
  selection, or everything.
- Every edit goes to the groom at once and the hair follows; **Ctrl+Z** undoes it, the graph too.
- The **Node** tab of the editor's sidebar (**N**), the Node Settings, edits the selected node as
  Maya's param panel does: its name, type and what it does; its params by group (click a group's title to fold it,
  for every node of that type), **Advanced** folded; sliders over each param's usual range, which
  you can type past; lengths in scene units. **Ramps** are edited with Blender's curve widget. A
  param set by a painted map or a texture shows the map's name. A node saved with an older
  behaviour offers **Upgrade**; a note (ⓘ) says when a node does nothing as set, errors show in
  red.
- **Presets** in the header (and the sidebar's GroomLab tab) lists the presets by category: each
  one can be applied (the graph replaced, fitted to your scalp) or inserted after the selected
  node as one Compound node, its key params on the Compound itself.
- The editor's **⋯** menu has **Tidy Layout** (columns from the sources to the output) and
  **Reload from Groom**.

Node positions are saved with the groom, where Maya's and the app's editors keep them: a groom laid
out in Blender opens laid out the same in Maya, and the other way round.

## Animation

GroomLab follows a deforming scalp, from an armature or shape keys: on each frame it reads the
mesh as Blender deforms it. With **Follow Scalp** on (the default) the groom is made on the scalp's
rest pose and each strand is carried along by its root, so the hair keeps its shape as the head
turns. Groom at a frame where the head is at rest.

Modifiers that change the mesh's vertex count (Subdivision Surface, Decimate...) can't be
followed: GroomLab then grows on the mesh before its modifiers.

## Rendering

Cycles and EEVEE render the hair object like any other Blender Curves object. A final render (F12,
or an animation) uses every strand: GroomLab switches the hair object to full density for the
render and back to the preview density afterwards, and turns on **Render > Lock Interface** for
that switch.

Each point's width (a diameter) is written as Blender's per-point `radius` (half the width), so
Cycles and EEVEE render the same coverage as Arnold to within about a percent. EEVEE needs
**Render > Curves > Shape** set to **Strip**; its default, Strand, draws every hair one pixel wide
regardless of width. GroomLab sets Strip when you create or open a groom, and the panel warns if it
has been set back to Strand.

The hair material reproduces GroomLab's Arnold hair shader with Blender's **Principled Hair BSDF**,
driven by the same HairColor, Bands, FeatherColor and RenderData nodes:

- **Pigment**: each strand's melanin and redness, with the node's dye over them.
- **RGB**: when the color comes from more than the pigment (a tip color, a map, hue, saturation
  or value randomness, a Bands or Feather Color node, a Blend of two looks), the strands' color,
  per point along them.
- **Both**, mixed strand by strand by its share, when the Hair Color node sets a Color Source; the
  pigment's look then varies in hue, saturation and value per strand as the RGB does.
- **Iridescence**: the structural color shifting with the viewing angle, mixed in where the
  Feather Color node puts it.
- **Highlight**: HairColor's specular and roughness (roughness per strand where it
  varies), and its opacity.

Arnold's and Cycles' hair models parameterize melanin differently, so a **GroomLab Hair Color**
node group converts melanin, redness, dye and color to the values that render in Cycles as they do
in Arnold (measured on the same grooms: average hair color within about 2-5%, comparable highlight
brightness).

The hair object carries these attributes, readable in your own shaders with an **Attribute** node.
Per-strand values use Blender's **Curve** domain, per-point values the **Point** domain:

| Attribute | Domain, type | Contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `radius` | Point, float | Half the strand width (Blender's own radius attribute) |
| `groomlab_strand_id` | Curve, 2 x int32 | The 64-bit strand ID, split into low and high 32 bits |
| `surface_uv_coordinate` | Curve, float2 | The scalp UV at the root (Blender's standard name) |
| `groomlab_color` | Curve, color | Linear RGB per strand |
| `groomlab_point_color` | Point, color | Linear RGB per point, when the color varies along the strand |
| `melanin`, `melaninRedness`, `specular`, `roughness`, `opacity`, `iridescence` | Curve or Point (as the graph wrote them), float | HairColor, RenderData and FeatherColor values, when the graph writes them |
| `groomlab_root_normal` | Curve, vector | The root normal used for iridescence (only with FeatherColor iridescence) |

Only the attributes in this table reach Blender. Other strand attributes (`clumpId`, Group masks,
Attribute node output) are not written to the hair object yet; export Alembic or USD to get them.
GroomLab does not modify a material you assign yourself.

What's approximate:

- **EEVEE** shades hair as a flat color without highlights or transmission. GroomLab gives it the
  color that matches Cycles' average, which is close for a full head of hair and slightly bright
  for light, sparse fur. Use Cycles to compare with Arnold.
- A HairColor **specular** below 0.2 renders a little shinier than in Arnold, and **iridescence**
  doesn't tint the highlight as Arnold's does.
- Compare colors with **Color Management > View Transform: Standard** (Blender's default, AgX,
  tones the image differently from Arnold's sRGB output).


## Files

- **Save Groom File...** writes the groom in GroomLab's file conventions (Y up, centimeters). Maya
  and the GroomLab app open it as evaluated in Blender, with the same guides.
- **Open Groom File...** opens a groom from Maya, the app or Blender on the selected mesh, or with the
  scalp stored in the file (**Use the File's Scalp**) when you don't have the head in Blender.
- A **.blend** file stores the whole groom (guides, groups, maps, the selection), so reopening it
  restores the hair and the Groom Tools state.

## The GroomLab menu

The top bar's **GroomLab** menu has Maya's GroomLab menu items: **Groom Tools**, **Graph Editor**,
**Create Groom from Selection**, **Groom Files** (open and save `.groom`), **Paint**, **Presets**,
**Export** (Alembic, USD, Groom File (.groom)), **Keyboard Shortcuts...**, **Open Download Page**
(updates come through Blender's extensions), **Help** (Docs, Keyboard Shortcuts..., About
GroomLab) and **Support GroomLab**. Blender's **File > Import** and **File > Export** have
**GroomLab Groom File (.groom)** too.

## Export

**Alembic** and **USD** export the strands (the current frame or the scene's range, every strand
by default) in GroomLab's space: Y up, centimeters for Alembic, `metersPerUnit = 0.01` for USD.
Blender's importers convert both. The files carry the same attributes as Maya's exports
([Strand attributes](/docs/rendering#strand-attributes)).

## Not in Blender yet

- **Jiggle** and **Simulate** nodes: the panel marks them. Jiggle passes the strands through in
  Blender, and Simulate isn't fed the scalp's motion frame by frame, so don't rely on either in a
  Blender render yet.
- The Groom Tools' **Correctives** and **Photos** tabs, and **Guides from Photos
  (experimental)...**: use Maya or the GroomLab app; grooms carrying correctives evaluate in
  Blender as made.
- **Transfer Groom to Scalp...** (Maya and the app) and the **XGen** and **Yeti** importers (Maya):
  transfer or import there, save a `.groom` file and open it in Blender.
- Grooms made with earlier previews keep their guides as a Blender Curves object you sculpt with
  Blender's Curves Sculpt brushes (**Sculpt Guides**); make a new groom for the Groom Tools.
- macOS and Linux builds.
- Keep the scalp object's scale at 1 (apply it): presets fit the groom to the mesh's own size.

## What's open source

All of it. The add-on (its Python code) is free software under GPL-3.0-or-later. Its source is
included in the extension. GroomLab's engine runs in `groomlab-helper`, a separate program the
add-on starts and talks to. It is never loaded into Blender. The helper is open source too, under
the Apache License 2.0 (`bin/LICENSE-HELPER.txt` inside the extension), and its source is in
[GroomLab's repository](https://github.com/thekesslereffect/groomlab).
