GroomLab and Maya's XGen Interactive Grooming (Interactive Groom Splines) were timed on the same
scalp and machine, at 100,000 and 500,000 strands. The benchmark harness is in the GroomLab source
(`tools/bench/xgen_compare`), so you can rerun it on your own hardware.

<BenchmarkSetup />

## Summary

- **Creation and file size.** A new groom is evaluated in under a second, against about twelve
  seconds for XGen. A saved scene stores GroomLab's guides and graph, not its strands: a few MB at
  any strand count.
- **Animation and exports.** With [Follow Scalp](/docs/motion-and-simulation#follow-the-scalp) the
  groom is evaluated once on the rest pose and transformed by the scalp, so per-frame evaluation of
  an animated scalp, Alembic export and motion-blurred Arnold export take a fraction of XGen's
  time.
- **Interactive edits.** A parameter change redraws faster than XGen's at both strand counts, and
  the evaluation alone takes about half of XGen's time. While a slider or brush is dragged, the
  preview is evaluated on the GPU: about 12 ms to the redrawn frame at 500,000 strands, against
  132 ms for an XGen tweak; on release, the result is computed on the CPU.

**Follow Scalp** is GroomLab with Follow Scalp on, for the measurements with an animated scalp.
**No Curl** is the benchmark groom without its Curl node, so it runs the same operators as XGen
here (clump and noise), for the closest comparison. **GPU preview** is GroomLab while a slider or
brush is being dragged: the preview is evaluated on the GPU, and the CPU result on release
differs from it in under 0.1% of pixels. Measured with Viewport 2.0 on OpenGL, as for XGen.

## How it was measured

- Every measurement runs in a fresh Maya: `mayapy` for batch work (creation, tweaks, exports,
  per-frame evaluation without drawing), the Maya GUI for the viewport (the window pinned to
  1600 x 1000, the perspective view alone, smooth shaded, default lighting).
- **Creation** is from nothing to a fully evaluated groom (XGen's `xgmCreateSplineDescription`
  with its clump and noise modifiers; GroomLab's `create_groom`, guides from curves and its
  graph). In the GUI, to the first drawn frame.
- **Tweaks** change the clump strength or noise amplitude, then force an evaluation (batch) or a
  redraw (GUI).
- **Animated scalp**: a bend deformer keyed over 100 frames; per-frame evaluation, playback and
  exports over its frames.
- **Arnold**: `.ass` export time, static and with 2-key motion blur.
- **Memory**: the process's working set after creation; GPU memory is the whole device's change
  (`nvidia-smi`).

## Caveats

- The two systems do different work. XGen evaluates each spline through its modifiers; GroomLab
  scatters strands, interpolates them from about 500 guides and runs its graph. Clump and noise
  algorithms and units differ, and GroomLab's clumps are tighter at the same strength.
- XGen 2022 has no per-strand curl, so the matched comparison is **No Curl**.
- XGen's brushes take only mouse events (they cannot be scripted), so brush timings are
  GroomLab's alone.
- XGen uses OpenCL in the GUI; GroomLab evaluates on the CPU, multithreaded across all cores
  (except the GPU preview during drags).

## Run it yourself

With a GroomLab build and Maya 2022 (and MtoA for the render part):

```bash
"C:/Program Files/Autodesk/Maya2022/bin/mayapy.exe" tools/bench/xgen_compare/run_bench.py --runs 5
```

`--counts`, `--parts batch,render,gui` and `--systems` pick what runs; the results go to
`tools/bench/xgen_compare/results/` as JSON and a Markdown table. A full run takes two to three
hours. The harness README documents every option.
