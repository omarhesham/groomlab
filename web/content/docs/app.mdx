<Callout type="info" title="Beta">
  The standalone app is in beta on Windows. It is usable, but details may change before the first
  release. Keep backups of important grooms, and report problems to
  [support@getgroomlab.com](mailto:support@getgroomlab.com) with the log file (see below).
</Callout>

The GroomLab app grooms, lights and exports grooms without Maya. It uses the same evaluation core
as the Maya plug-in, so a `.groom` file evaluates to the same strands in both.

## Quick start

Your first groom, from a head mesh to an exported file. [Install](#install) the app first.

<Steps>
<Step>

### Start a groom on a mesh

Choose **File > New Groom on Mesh...** (Ctrl+N), or **New groom on mesh** on the start card, and
pick the head as an `.obj` or `.abc` file. The groom starts with the default graph and no guides,
so it has no hair until you place some. (A `.groom` file from Maya or Blender opens with **File >
Open Groom...**, Ctrl+O.)

</Step>
<Step>

### Place guides

Pick **Place** on the brush rail at the left and click or drag on the scalp. Shape the guides with
**Comb**. Hold **B** and drag to size the brush. For more detail, open **Guide groups** on the
right rail and click **Generate children**.

</Step>
<Step>

### Pick a look

Click the **Presets** pill at the bottom, open a category, pick a preset and choose **Apply**. The
preset is fitted to your scalp and grows from your guides. For the hair color, switch to **Look**
mode (Ctrl+2) and use the **Material** tab.

</Step>
<Step>

### Tweak it in the graph

Press **Tab** (or click the **Graph** pill). Click a node, for example the Clump, and drag its
sliders in **Node Settings**; the groom updates as you drag. **Tab** over the graph adds a node.

</Step>
<Step>

### Save and export

**File > Save** (Ctrl+S) writes a `.groom` file that Maya and Blender open. **Export** mode
(Ctrl+E) writes Alembic, USD, an Unreal groom, Unity strands or glTF cards.

</Step>
</Steps>

## Install

The app needs Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit) and a graphics card with Direct3D 11. There is no
installer:

1. [Download the GroomLab app](https://downloads.getgroomlab.com/app/GroomLab-App-windows-latest.zip)
   (also on the [Download](/download) page, with its SHA-256).
2. Unzip the whole folder anywhere you can write to, and run **GroomLab.exe** in it. Windows
   SmartScreen may warn about a new download: choose **More info**, then **Run anyway**.
3. It is free, with every feature and export enabled. **Help > Support GroomLab** opens the Ko-fi
   page.

**Help > Check for Updates...** reports a newer version. To update, download it, close GroomLab and
unzip it over the old folder; settings and recent files are kept. The log file for bug reports is
`%LOCALAPPDATA%\GroomLab\logs\GroomLab.log`. `README_BETA.txt` in the app folder repeats these
notes.

## Open a groom

With no groom open, the window shows **Open groom**, **New groom on mesh**, **Start from a
preset** and recent files; Render, the mode pills, Graph and Presets wait until a groom is open.
**Start from a preset** (or **Presets > Apply on a Mesh...**) asks for a mesh, makes the groom on
it and applies the look. After **New Groom on Mesh** a card suggests the next step: pick **Place**
and click the scalp, or open **Presets**. **View > Rail Labels** shows names beside the rail icons
(on for the first run).

- **File > Open Groom...** (Ctrl+O) opens a `.groom` file, saved here or from Maya.
- **File > New Groom on Mesh...** (Ctrl+N) starts a groom on an `.obj` or `.abc` mesh, with the
  default graph and no guides. An animated Alembic mesh becomes the groom's scalp cache, so the
  animation is kept.
- Or drop a `.groom`, `.obj` or `.abc` file on the window.
- **File > Add Prop Mesh...** loads another mesh beside the groom (a body, clothes, a hat). Props
  are drawn and lit with the groom, and a ColliderMesh node can name one to keep the strands off
  it ([Colliders](/docs/utilities#colliders)).
- **File > Add Eye...** adds an eye: a prompt over the viewport takes its center, the direction it looks and its radius
  (prefilled in front of the scene), as `--eye` does on the command line.
- **File > Save Image...** saves the current view as a PNG.

**File > Reload from File** (Ctrl+R) reads the saved file again. When the groom has unsaved
changes, Reload, closing the window, **Quit** (Ctrl+Q), Open, New and a dropped or recent file ask
first in a prompt over the viewport: **Save**, **Discard** or **Cancel**. **Tools > Transfer Groom to
Scalp...** moves the groom onto another mesh in the same two-view window as Maya's: pick a method,
then click marker pairs on both meshes or import them from a JSON file
([Utilities](/docs/utilities#transfer-to-a-new-scalp)). Open, New Groom on Mesh and Transfer show a
busy cursor and say what they are doing while they work. **Tools > Guides from Photos
(Experimental)...** grows guides from reference photos ([Guides from photos](/docs/reference-photos#guides-from-photos-experimental)),
and **Tools > Turntable** turns the camera around the groom.

## The layout

The viewport fills the window; all other UI is drawn over it.

| Where | What |
| --- | --- |
| Top left | The menu button (File, Edit, View, Tools, Help) and the file name. A dot means unsaved edits. |
| Top center | The modes: **Groom**, **Look**, **Cards** and **Export**. |
| Top right | Undo, redo and **Render**. |
| Left | The brush rail: every brush, then Select (Groom mode). |
| Right | The panel rail (Groom mode): **Guide groups**, **Node List**, **Maps**, **Correctives**, **Look Settings**, **Display** and **Frame all**. |
| Bottom | The brush bar (Groom mode), the stats, and the **Graph** and **Presets** pills. |

Both rails expand on hover to show button labels.

Each panel button toggles a floating panel beside it. Two other panels open from elsewhere:
**Brush**, from the brush bar's settings button, and **Node Settings**, when you select a node in
the Graph Editor.

A timeline appears at the bottom when the groom is animated (a scalp or guide cache, or a graph
that reads the time). It supports play, scrub and loop; **Follow** toggles
[follow-the-scalp](/docs/motion-and-simulation#follow-the-scalp). With a Simulate node in the
graph it also shows **Simulate**, **Bake** and **Clear**; while it runs, **Simulate** reads **Cancel** (the frames made stay in the cache).

## Groom with brushes

Pick a brush on the left rail. The rail holds the same brushes as Maya, in the same order:
Create, Shape, Trim and Paint, then **Select** and **Freeze**. See
[Guides and brushes](/docs/guides-and-brushes) for what each does.

- Drag on the groom to stroke. **Ctrl** reverses the brush; **Shift** smooths.
- **Freeze** paints a per-guide freeze mask; other brushes skip frozen guides, which draw blue.
  **Ctrl** unfreezes; **Unfreeze All** in the brush panel clears the mask.
- **Shift+click** a guide to solo it (all other guides freeze) and switch the brush to its level;
  Shift+click off a guide to unfreeze all. **Page Up** and **Page Down** step the level. **Solo new
  guides** in the panel solos guides as you place them; Select mode solos the selection.
- Hold **B** and drag, or middle-drag, to set the radius. Hold **M** and drag, or
  **Shift+middle-drag**, to set the strength.
- **Alt+Shift+X** turns symmetry on or off.
- **Alt** with any mouse button moves the camera, whatever the tool.
- **Esc** leaves the brush for the camera.

The brush bar at the bottom holds the radius, strength, falloff, **Symmetry** and pen pressure. Its
settings button opens the **Brush** panel with every setting.

**Guide groups** lists the guide groups. Untick a group to hide it; double-click to rename it. The
panel's buttons add a root or child group, generate children, select or remove a group, delete
the selected guides, mirror a group +X → -X, sync symmetry, promote a group and resample guides.
Part lines and flow arrows drawn with the Part and Flow brushes are listed there too. See
[Guide groups](/docs/guides-and-brushes#guide-groups).

## The Graph Editor

Press **Tab** or click the **Graph** pill to open the Graph Editor. The graph is drawn over the
viewport, with the groom visible behind it.

- **Tab** over the graph searches for a node to add, by name, by what it does (symmetry finds
  Mirror, frizz finds Noise) or by category. **Enter** adds it, wired after the selected node when
  their ports match.
- Drag from an output port to an input to wire them. Drag a wire off an input to disconnect it.
- Click a node to select it; its params open in the **Node Settings** panel. **Ctrl+click**
  toggles a node in the selection, **Shift+click** adds one, and dragging on empty space selects
  in a box; dragging a selected node moves them all. **Delete** and **B** act on the whole
  selection; **Ctrl+C** / **Ctrl+V** copy and paste the selected nodes with the links between them.
- **V** (**View Up to Here**) shows the groom up to the selected node instead of the output's
  (the node says VIEWING; **V** again goes back). Exports and renders still use the output.
- A node that fails to evaluate gets a red ring and a **!** badge; its error is in its tooltip and
  under the title in Node Settings.
- Dragging a link over a port it can't feed says why; dropping it there changes nothing.
- **Ctrl+G** groups the selected nodes into one **Compound** node, as in Maya's Graph Editor: the
  links from outside become its inputs, and one of the nodes must feed the rest of the graph.
  Double-click a Compound (or select it and press **Ctrl+Shift+G**) to open it: its nodes go back
  into the graph where they were. **Ctrl+Z** undoes either in one step.
- **Backdrops** frame part of the graph: right-click a node > **Add Backdrop Around Selection**, or
  right-click the canvas > **Add Backdrop**, and type its title. Drag its title bar to move it with
  the nodes on it, drag its corner to resize it, double-click the title to rename it, right-click it
  for **Color** and **Delete Backdrop** (or select it and press **Delete**).
- Right-click a node to rename, duplicate, bypass or delete it, or set it as the output.
  Right-click the canvas to add a node or a backdrop, tidy the layout or frame the graph.
- Middle-drag pans; scroll to zoom. **F** frames the selected node (none: the whole graph),
  **A** the whole graph. Alt+drag moves the camera.

Close the graph with **Esc**, the close button beside the pills, or by clicking a mode. Each edit
is one undo step. See [Node graph](/docs/node-graph) for the nodes.

### Presets

The **Presets** pill (or **View > Graph Presets**) lists the presets by category; hover one for
its description. Each preset has two actions:

- **Apply** replaces the graph with the preset.
- **Insert after selected node** keeps your graph and adds the preset's look as one Compound
  node after the selected node, or before the output when nothing is selected.

In both cases the preset's lengths and densities are scaled to your scalp, any maps it reads are
created, and the change is one undo step. The same menu has **Save as Preset...**, **Copy Graph**
and **Paste Graph** (the graph as JSON text, to share or paste onto another groom). See
[Presets](/docs/presets).

### Parameters

Click a node in the **Node List** panel or in the graph to edit it.

- Params are grouped under collapsible headers. Rarely used params are in **Advanced**, collapsed.
- A param is hidden while the node's other settings make it inactive.
- Numbers show their unit (percentage, degrees, turns, seconds or pixels).
- Ramps have a curve editor. Mappable params can take a constant, a painted map, a texture, a
  color set, an expression or a field.
- **Bypass** passes the node's input through unchanged.
- **Upgrade** appears beside the title when the node was saved with an older version of its
  operator. It switches the node to the newest behaviour, as one undo step.

Dragging a slider updates the groom during the drag. With **GPU preview** on (Display panel), the
GPU evaluates the preview during the drag and the CPU evaluation runs on release.

## Painting maps

Open **Maps**. **New map** creates a vertex map, a texture map or a guide layer, grayscale or
color. Pick a map and click **Paint this map**; the brush paints toward the set value or color,
**Ctrl** toward 0 on a grayscale map (toward 1 when the value is 0) and toward the inverse color on
a color map. **Show on scalp** displays the map on the scalp.

To paint a setting, set **New map**'s **For** to **Density**, **Width**, **Length**, **Clump
strength**, **Curl**, **Noise amplitude**, **Flyaways**, or **Other setting...** (any mappable
param of the graph), pick vertex or texture map, and click **Create**: a white map is created,
connected to the setting, selected and painted with the paint brush, in one undo step (if the
setting already reads a map, that map is selected instead). Strokes paint toward the value (1: the
setting's full value); **Ctrl** paints toward 0, so Ctrl on the density map removes hair. A
mappable param's **Paint** pill does the same for that param. See
[Painting maps](/docs/painting-maps).

## Correctives

**Correctives** holds shot-sculpt layers applied over the animated groom. A **New layer** applies
on every frame; **Fix this frame** creates a layer at full weight on the current frame that fades
in and out over six frames. Pick a layer under **Sculpt into** to send brush strokes to it instead
of the guides.

## Look

**Look** mode shows the look card, with three tabs:

- **Material:** hair color swatches (the groom's own, black, brown, blonde, red, white, dyed),
  HairColor's two ways (**Natural**: **Melanin** and **Redness**; **Picked color**: a **Color**),
  **Roughness**, **Tip color** and **Tip strength**, **Hue random** and **Saturation random**.
  These edit the groom's HairColor node, so they are saved with the groom. **More settings** shows every hair material setting and the other surfaces
  (scalp, props, eyes).
- **Lighting:** presets (studio, daylight, overcast, sunset, rim), exposure, the lights, an
  environment, the tone map and the background.
- **Render:** interactive or refine quality, refine samples and resolution scale.

**Render** at the top right restarts progressive rendering; the viewport accumulates samples until
it converges; **File > Save Image...** then saves the view as a PNG. The **Look Settings** panel on the right rail holds every look setting, including
texture files.

**Display** (right rail) sets what the viewport draws: preview density, the point budget, look-dev
shading, GPU preview, strand colors (solid, color set, attribute, random, clump, density), guides,
the scalp and a map on the scalp. The point budget defaults to 300k points to keep large grooms
interactive; 0 means no limit.

## Game cards

**Cards** mode generates hair cards from the groom. Click **Add Cards node** if the graph has
none.

- **Strands** or **Cards** switches what the viewport shows. **Maps** shows the baked maps on the
  cards, alpha-tested by coverage as in Maya and in a game engine. Before a bake the cards are
  untextured.
- **LOD** picks **Hero**, **Mid**, **Low** or **Custom**. Sliders set cards per clump,
  layers, width, segments, variations, the atlas size and a card budget.
- **Bake maps** writes the cards' maps (depth, coverage, tangent, attribute, color, occlusion) as
  TGA files in a `_cards` folder beside the saved groom file, with a busy cursor while it bakes. The
  baked atlas shows in the panel.

The panel shows card, triangle and texture counts, and indicates when the cards have changed since
the last bake. See [Game export](/docs/game-export).

## Export

**Export** mode (Ctrl+E) writes the groom to other formats. Pick a target:

| Target | What you get |
| --- | --- |
| **Alembic curves** | Every strand with its ID, attributes, color and root UV ([Strand attributes](/docs/rendering#strand-attributes)). |
| **USD curves** | BasisCurves in a text `.usda` layer. |
| **Unreal groom** | Alembic for Unreal's groom importer, with the guides it simulates. |
| **Cards (glTF)** | The cards at this frame as `.glb` (or `.gltf`), maps inside, with the hair cap. |
| **Unity strands** | Alembic for Unity's hair package, resampled to a fixed point count. |

Set the frames (the current frame, or a range for an animated groom), the density and the target's
options, then the output file (type a path or pick it with **...**). **Export** shows progress and
can be cancelled. FBX card export is only available in Maya.

## Save and open in Maya

**File > Save** (Ctrl+S) and **File > Save As...** (Ctrl+Shift+S) write a `.groom` file: the graph,
guides, scalp, maps, parts, flow and correctives. Export mode has **Save** and **Save as...** too.

In Maya, choose **GroomLab > Groom Files > Import Groom File...**. Select the scalp mesh first if
the scene has it; otherwise GroomLab creates the scalp from the file. To pull later edits into a
groom linked to the file, save in the app, then choose **GroomLab > Groom Files > Reload from
Groom File** in Maya (one undo step). See [The .groom format](/docs/groom-format).

## Not in the app yet

These are in Maya but not in the app. Grooms that use them still open and evaluate in the app as
made.

- **Import:** **From XGen...** and **From Yeti...** ([Importing](/docs/importing)). Alembic curves
  come in through an **ImportCurves** node.
- **Rendering:** Arnold, RenderMan, Redshift and V-Ray, and **Render Turntable...**. The app's
  **Render** refines the viewport; export Alembic or USD for other renderers.
- **Exports:** the FBX ones (**Unreal Cards...**, **Unity Cards...**, **Unity Feathers...**,
  **Scales Mesh (FBX)...**) and **Scales Instancer (USD)...**. The app bakes the card maps and
  writes glTF cards.
- **Graph Editor:** display units, **Drive with Slider**, and the expression editor's completion
  and library.
- **Maps:** **Convert to**, **Make Color** / **Make Gray**, **Fill** on an existing map, **Import
  Image as Map...**, **Relink Image...**, **Reload Image** and "Read by".
- **Guides:** **Reparent...** and demoting a group (the app promotes), group colors and **Hue per group**, **Isolate**,
  locking child guides, and the Select tool's soft radius.
- **Correctives:** reordering and soloing layers, and keyed layer weights.
- **Animation:** keys on params, and Maya's nHair (**Simulate > Legacy**). The app plays a groom
  file's caches and runs the Simulate node.
- **Utilities:** **Bake Node to Guides...**, **Find Missing Files...**, **Strands to Curves...**,
  **Strands to Mesh...**, **Bake Scale Maps** and **Copy Graph to Other Grooms...** (in the app,
  **Copy Graph** and **Paste Graph** under Presets carry a look between grooms).
- More than one groom in a window.

## Themes

**View > Theme** picks the app's colors from the same themes as Maya: **Playful** (the default:
translucent panels, bright primaries), **Charcoal** (solid neutral grey panels with thin borders,
an off-white primary button, muted color only where it means something), **Polar** and
**Studio**. The choice is saved and the app wears it the next time it starts. A scripted run takes
`--theme charcoal` (or another name) for that run alone. The app reads only the shipped themes;
custom ones are made in Maya (see [Themes](/docs/utilities#themes)).
