GroomLab is in early access, and it changes quickly. This page lists the improvements that change
how you work, newest first. It will keep growing as the system is fleshed out. Something missing,
or something you would like to see? Write to
[support@getgroomlab.com](mailto:support@getgroomlab.com).

**Check for Updates** in the GroomLab menu (the app: **Help > Check for Updates**) gets the latest
version. While GroomLab is in early access, a node's params can still change between updates (a
param renamed, or two merged into one); the entries below say when one does and what to set. Once
it leaves early access, saved grooms will keep their look across updates.

## 0.19.0 (October 19, 2026)

- **Use GroomLab from AI agents.** `groomlab-cli mcp` is an MCP server, so Claude Desktop, Claude
  Code, Cursor, VS Code and other AI apps can build grooms through GroomLab's tools: new grooms on a
  mesh or a sample, presets, nodes and params, guides and tubes, script nodes, a preview image the
  agent looks at, `.groom` files and Alembic or USD export. It works in one folder you choose, makes
  no network connections, and every change can be undone. See
  [Use GroomLab from AI agents](/docs/ai-agents).
- **Card map exports.** **Export Maps...** on the Cards node (the app: Export > **Card maps**;
  Blender: **Card Maps...**) writes the cards' textures as your engine wants them, in one click:
  pick a preset (Unreal Hair Cards, Unreal packed, Unity HDRP Hair, or every map on its own), a
  resolution up to 8K, and PNG, TGA, TIFF or EXR at 8, 16 or 32 bits, with automatic edge padding
  and optional supersampling. New maps: a normal map (OpenGL or DirectX), per-strand seed,
  per-clump ID, root to tip, specular, translucency and a flow map, beside depth, coverage,
  tangent, color and AO. **Advanced** packs any map into any R, G, B or A channel, inverted or
  not; save the packing as a preset that Maya, the app and Blender share
  ([Export card maps](/docs/game-export#export-card-maps)).
- **Studio lighting.** **GroomLab > Studio** (or **Studio** in the Groom Tools' **Output** section)
  puts a photo studio round the groom in one click: a seamless grey backdrop, Arnold softbox lights,
  an 85 mm three-quarter camera and a matte grey mannequin head. Five presets (Portrait, Beauty,
  Rim Drama, High Key, Turntable) relight it in place; it scales with the groom, and **Remove Studio**
  puts back the head's materials and the render settings ([Studio lighting](/docs/rendering#studio-lighting)).

## 0.18.0 (October 18, 2026)

- **Compound nodes.** Right-click a compound > **Save as Node...** to keep it in your node library
  as a node of your own: it shows in **Add Node** and **Tab** search under its category, in every
  groom, in Maya, the app and Blender. Share it as one `.glnode` file (**Import Node...**, or drop
  it on the Graph Editor). Grooms carry a copy of the nodes they use, so they open and render
  anywhere; a newer version in your library shows an update mark on the node. Three examples ship:
  Soft Frizz, Grey Streaks and Wind-swept Ends ([Custom nodes](/docs/custom-nodes)).
- **Script nodes.** **Add Node > New Script Node** makes a node of your own from a short script in
  the expression language: it runs per strand and per point, moves points, sets widths and colors
  and writes channels, with parameters of its own (sliders, mappable, ramps). Its editor opens in
  the Graph Editor's side panel and is live: the groom follows as you type, and errors say their
  line and column. Save it to your library and share it as one `.glnode` file, like a compound
  node. Four examples ship: Wave, Kink, Taper by Channel and Random Length. In the app, double-click
  a script node to edit it; Blender lists and evaluates them ([Script
  nodes](/docs/custom-nodes#script-nodes)).

## 0.17.1 (October 18, 2026)

- **Tubes has its own section** in Groom Tools > Tools: shapes, sizes and the shape library in one
  place, opened by the Shape brush, Place and Draw, or any time from its header.
- **Fixes:** Scatter's guide radius fills a sized round tube's footprint (only shaped tubes did);
  the Tubes section edits the guide of a groom that has just one; the **Tube locks** preset feeds
  its guides to Scatter (guide radius 1), so hair grows only on the tubes.

## 0.17.0 (October 18, 2026)

- **Join us on Discord.** A GroomLab community for questions, work in progress and feedback:
  [discord.gg/sr2rxjRVkm](https://discord.gg/sr2rxjRVkm), also under **Help > Discord** in Maya and
  the app, and in the Blender menu's About.
- **Tube guides: shape the hair as whole locks.** Every guide's tube now has a shape (round,
  ribbon, teardrop or blade) and sections along it (width, aspect and twist, root to
  tip). Children are made inside the shape and follow it as it flattens, twists and tapers.
  Interpolate's new **Fill Tubes** fills each tube with strands that stay inside it to the tip, and
  Clump's **Follow Tubes** (on) clumps them inside their own tube: anime and cartoon hair without
  modelling a mesh. In Python: `set_tube` and `get_tube`; draw them with Display Tubes. See
  [Tube guides](/docs/guides-and-brushes#tube-guides).
- **The Shape brush shapes tubes in Maya.** It replaces the Tube brush (same place in Groom Tools >
  Tools > Shape): drag over guides to paint their tubes' **Width**, **Flatness** or **Twist** near
  the brush, point by point along each guide (Ctrl does the opposite). Its card sets the selected
  guides' tube **Shape**, **Width**, **Flatness** and **Twist** as a whole, and the groom's **Fill
  Tubes**. While it is on, the tubes draw as wires, a section outline at every guide point. In
  Python: `brush_stroke(..., "shape", tube_channel=...)`, `scale_tube`, `set_fill_tubes`.
- **Fill Tubes has a soft edge and fills every level.** Interpolate's new **Tube Blend** (25% of a
  tube's half-width) blends the strands just outside a tube into it, so no seam shows round a lock.
  And the tubes of every guide level fill now (a child's tube inside its parent's takes its own
  strands, the parent's the rest), without setting the Guides node's level.
- **Tubes fill at any angle.** Strands no longer collapse into a thin band when a guide leaves the
  head at a shallow angle (lying along it): a tube's footprint is now its section laid flat on the
  scalp, so its strands fill the whole shape (blade, ribbon, teardrop, round, flat or not) at any
  angle. Picking a shape lays the tube flat on the head (twist 0: its width along the scalp).
- **Shaping tubes is quicker.** While the Shape brush is on, every guide it can shape shows its
  tube (the selected ones brighter), with lines along each tube's corners so a blade reads as a
  blade. **Shift+drag** with it smooths the channel it paints along the guides; the Tubes card's
  new **Smooth** button does the same for the selected guides (the current channel or all three;
  `smooth_tube` in Python). The card edits only the selected guides and shows **Mixed** when their
  shapes differ.
- **Custom tube shapes.** Draw your own cross-sections: each groom has a **shape library** saved with
  its guides (in the scene and in `.groom` files), and guides take a library shape by name like a
  built-in one. Fill Tubes, children, clumps and the wires follow its outline exactly. The Tubes
  card has a **shape editor**: a square with the outline's points to drag (the groom follows live;
  letting go saves, one undo step), click the outline to add a point, right-click one to remove it,
  **Mirror** (on) keeps both sides alike, **Smooth** or straight edges. **Edit as Custom** copies a
  built-in into the library; **New**, **Rename**, **Duplicate**, **Delete** and **Apply to Selected**
  manage it, with no dialogs. Editing a shape updates every guide using it. Outlines that cross
  themselves or leave out their center are refused with a message. In Python: `add_tube_shape`,
  `set_tube_shape_points`, `rename_tube_shape`, `remove_tube_shape`, `tube_shapes`, and
  `set_tube(..., shape="<name>")`. See [Tube guides](/docs/guides-and-brushes#tube-guides).
- **New guides take the shape you chose.** Place and Draw (and their mirrors) give a new guide the
  shape picked in the Tubes card (a built-in or a library shape), and blend its width, flatness and
  twist from the two nearest guides; with none near, it takes the card's Width, Flatness and Twist.
  The card shows for Place and Draw too. In Python: `brush_tool(..., tube={...})`,
  `brush_stroke(..., tube={...})`, `placement_tube()`.
- **Tube sections follow the head.** A shaped tube's sections turn to lie as the scalp does all
  along the guide (twist 0: width along the scalp), so a ribbon combed round the head stays flat on
  it; off the head they carry on as they left it.
- **The Tubes card follows solos.** With no guides selected it edits the soloed ones (Shift+click a
  guide with a brush), and it follows every change of the selection or solo.
- **Children of slanted tubes spread out.** Children of a shaped tube whose guide leaves the head at
  a slant root across its footprint instead of bunching on a line (upright guides and plain round
  tubes keep theirs).
- **Scatter's guideRadius fills tube footprints.** Round a guide with a shaped tube, Scatter keeps
  the roots on the tube's footprint (plus the new **tubeMargin**) instead of a disc, so blades and
  ribbons fill evenly.
- **Tube locks (anime) preset.** A start for stylized hair (Presets > Hair): Fill Tubes with a soft
  edge, soft clumps that follow the tubes, a little noise and tapered tips. Place guides, then
  shape their tubes with the Shape brush.
- The teardrop shape is round against the scalp and points away from it, and the crescent shape is gone (a
  saved crescent loads as round).

Grooms saved before: guide tubes return to the default width (0.1); set them again with `set_tube`
or the Shape brush. Scripts naming the `tube` brush mode use `shape` now.

## 0.16.0 (October 17, 2026)

- **A wolf to start from.** **GroomLab > Add Sample Mesh** now offers a sitting wolf (body, eyes,
  nails and mouth, its body selected for Create Groom from Selection) beside Nika. Free to use,
  commercially too, with credit: "Wolf by Nick Rutlinh (GroomLab), CC BY 4.0". Add Sample Head
  (Nika) is now Add Sample Mesh > Nika (head), and in Python `add_sample("nika")` or
  `add_sample("wolf")` (was `add_sample_head()`).
- **A painted map works wherever you pick it.** Choosing a map painted on the scalp's vertices
  from a param's map menu (Painted map) no longer fails with "no guide carries the layer".
- **More drags preview live on the GPU.** Dragging a param of a node feeding a Merge's second input
  (a whisker or eyebrow branch merged with the fur), and Collide's **Offset**, now update instantly
  instead of waiting for a full evaluation.
- **Faster simulation.** The Simulate node steps nearly three times as fast (5,000 guides: about
  17 ms a frame instead of 48 on a 12-core machine), with exactly the same results.
- **A bake is what played.** Baking (and Simulate Range) now gives the simulation you watched:
  frames that real-time playback skipped are spread evenly instead of making the hair whip, and
  they are kept when you scrub back; Simulate Range meets colliders and other scalps where they
  are on each frame (it met them where they stood on the current frame); and a bake of guides the
  graph makes (ToGuides, imported curves) follows the head with Follow Scalp on. For the closest
  match, set Maya's playback to **Play every frame** while you simulate.
- **Hair drapes over ears.** Interpolate's **Keep Clearance** now only lifts strands out of the
  skin and lets them return to their groomed path after an ear or a cheek, so hair drapes over ears
  instead of standing off them (no more gap below the ear).
- **A documented, versioned USD layout.** Every USD export (Maya, the app, Blender,
  `groomlab-cli`) now follows one specification, [USD layout](/docs/usd-layout): every prim,
  attribute and primvar with its type, interpolation and units, and how to read one frame or a
  range. Each file carries its version (`groomlab:layoutVersion` 1, in the layer's
  `customLayerData` and the root prim's `customData`), so viewers and pipeline tools can build on
  it. The app's USD export now also writes the scales and the groom file's prototype meshes.

- **Sculpt edits survive grouping.** Grouping a Sculpt node into a compound (Ctrl+G in Maya's Graph
  Editor or the app's node graph) no longer throws its brushed edits away: they move into the
  compound with the node, in the same undo step, and come back out with Ungroup. Renaming or
  duplicating a compound takes the edits of the Sculpt nodes inside it along.
- **Blender: painted maps show again after reloading.** Opening a groom file again onto the same
  mesh shows its painted map on the scalp at once (the first redraws failed and left the old map
  shown). After Blender's own undo or redo, the Paint tab's map list (and the guide groups) show
  the groom's maps as they are, instead of the list from before.

Grooms saved before: strands crossing ears and cheeks sit closer to the skin.

Exports: in USD, a scales instancer's (`scaleInstances`) per-instance root color is now
`primvars:scaleRootColor` (was `primvars:scaleColor`), the name the scales mesh uses for it; an
attribute named `normals` is written as `primvars:normals_2`.

## 0.15.0 (October 17, 2026)

- **Simulated hair no longer flips at the ends.** In fast head motion the ends of simulated guides
  no longer flip or jitter, at any substeps (more substeps used to make it worse). The Simulate
  node now solves each guide's stretch and bend as a whole, so stiff guides still hold their shape.
- **Simulated hair keeps its groom by default.** **Groomed Under Gravity** is now on for new
  Simulate nodes (and the Default preset): the groomed shape is taken as how the hair rests under
  gravity, so pressing play no longer drops the groom; only motion moves it. Turn it off to let
  gravity pull on the groomed shape.
- **Nika, a sample head to start from.** **GroomLab > Add Sample Head (Nika)** (and a button in
  Groom Tools' empty state) adds a head and shoulders at the origin, sized for the scene's units and
  selected: Create Groom from Selection grows hair on her. Free to use, commercially too, with
  credit: "Nika head by Nick Rutlinh (GroomLab), CC BY 4.0".
- **Arnold in Maya 2024.** The Maya 2024 build now renders grooms in Arnold like every other
  version.
- **Painting no longer leaves the map showing.** After the Paint chip (or + New Map) starts the
  Paint brush, choosing another brush or tool (Q) puts the groom's look back.
- **For developers.** Adding a node is one command (`tools/dev/new_node.py`, see
  [docs/EXTENDING.md](https://github.com/thekesslereffect/groomlab/blob/main/docs/EXTENDING.md));
  the noise, hash and ramp math is one source shared by the CPU and the GPU preview, and an
  expression function is one table row
  ([docs/MATH.md](https://github.com/thekesslereffect/groomlab/blob/main/docs/MATH.md)). A
  skinned, animated test scene builds from `samples/skinned_bust`.

Grooms saved before: simulations look slightly different; the same `bend` keeps about the same
stiffness, and baked simulation caches still play as they were baked. A Simulate node saved with
Groomed Under Gravity off keeps it off; one saved at the old default now keeps its groom (turn it
off for the old sag).

## 0.14.0 (October 17, 2026)

- **Each branch sets its own strand resolution.** A Scatter now grows its strands with the most
  segments a Resample *after it* asks for (a Resample fed nothing sets the default; without either,
  8), not the most any Resample in the graph asks for. Fur no longer carries the 51 points a
  whisker branch's Resample asks for: lighter, and much faster to evaluate. The Graph Editor (and
  the app's parameter panel) says on each Scatter and Resample which Resample sets its resolution.
- **Faster edits and playback with Instance nodes.** Param drags upstream of an Instance preview on
  the GPU (the instances follow at release), Follow Scalp carries instanced grooms on the GPU, and
  the instances' meshes are built again only when their strands move.
- **Width and color edits stay quick.** A Width or HairColor change keeps the points of every node
  after it (Collide, Merge, Instance): only the widths and colors are made again.
- **Quicker exports.** An Alembic or USD export of the frame the viewport shows takes the viewport's
  evaluation instead of evaluating the groom again, and a one-frame USD file is written in one pass.
- **Compounds and backdrops in the app.** The app's Graph Editor groups the selected nodes into a
  Compound (**Ctrl+G**) and opens one again (double-click, or **Ctrl+Shift+G**), one undo step
  each, exactly as Maya's Graph Editor does. Backdrops can be added, renamed, recolored, moved with
  their nodes, resized and deleted. The app's Node Settings panel takes the window's height when it
  needs it, and the rail labels of a first run no longer cover the stats and the Graph pill.
- **Faster brushing and guide edits.** Guides are re-made only for the group a stroke touches,
  Merge updates only the side that changed, and Follow Scalp carries strands on every core. Brush
  strokes start warm after the previous one, and caches are freed when a scene closes.
- **Keep Clearance is much quicker.** Interpolate pushes strands clear of the scalp at the guides'
  sample points instead of every point (on the CPU and the GPU), Collide skips surfaces far from a
  strand, Merge previews on the GPU, and Scatter randomness edits are faster. The process caches
  hold at most 2 GB (`GROOMLAB_CACHE_LIMIT_MB` sets another limit).
- **Clearer feedback.** Long exports show progress (Esc cancels) and say when they're done; XGen
  and Yeti imports, Guides from Photos, Transfer and card bakes show they're busy; errors say what
  went wrong in plain words, and a node in error shows it under its header.
- **Easier to find things.** Add Node and Tab search show labels, categories and descriptions and
  find nodes by what they do ("symmetry" finds Mirror). Groom Tools has an **Output** section
  (simulate, export, render) and **Import > Alembic Curves...**; the Sculpt tab is now
  **Correctives**, the Reference tab **Photos**, and the Reference node **Linked Groom**. Symmetry
  is always visible in the Group row. A **Paint** chip on mappable params adds a map in one click.
  New docs page: [Common tasks](/docs/common-tasks).
- **One set of shortcuts.** Maya, the app and Blender share one shortcut table; tooltips show the
  keys, and the app gains M+drag, Alt+Shift+X, B to bypass, and F/A in the graph.
- **The app.** It asks before closing, opening or reloading over unsaved changes; success messages
  show; menus follow the theme. The graph gets multi-select, copy and paste, View Up to Here (V),
  error badges and link-refusal reasons. **File > Add Prop Mesh...**, **Add Eye...** and **Save
  Image...** are new, and empty states say what to do next.

Grooms saved before: a Resample now only sets the resolution of its own branch; add a Resample to a
branch that relied on another's.

## 0.13.0 (October 16, 2026)

- **Scale with scalp.** A groom now scales with its mesh like a child object: scale the head (its
  transform, a rig's root, or the mesh itself) and lengths, widths, clump sizes and noise scale with
  it, density adjusts so the strand count stays the same, and guides keep their shapes. The scale
  comes from the scalp's surface area at the animation's start frame, so **Freeze Transformations**
  changes nothing and animation doesn't make it wobble. Your values are never changed: scaling back
  gives the original groom. **Scale Source** (Surface Area, Transform Only or Off), a **Groom
  Scale** readout and **Set Scale Reference** are in the Attribute Editor's Inputs (and the app's
  Display panel). Surface Area survives Freeze Transformations; Transform Only survives remodelling
  the mesh. When a remodel changes the scalp's size under Surface Area, GroomLab says once how much
  the groom scaled. See [Scaling a groom](/docs/guides-and-brushes#scaling-a-groom).
- **Transfer: Fit groom to the new scalp's size.** A new option in the Transfer window
  (`transfer_groom(..., fit_size=True)`) treats the new scalp as the old one resized, so the groom
  scales with it; off (the default), it keeps the size shown.

Grooms saved before: they start at 1x the first time they're measured, so nothing changes until a
mesh is resized. Scenes whose rest mesh was captured before the head was resized change their strand
count once.

## 0.12.0 (October 16, 2026)

- **Expose any parameter.** Every parameter can now be exposed as an input (574 of 579; the rest
  define a node's own ports). Per-strand inputs (maps, fields) work as before; everything else takes
  a **Value** port, one value for the whole node. A new **Value** node (number, integer, on/off,
  vector, color, choice or text) can drive many parameters at once, and an Expression can output a
  single value from the frame or time. Exposed parameters become a compound's inputs. See
  [Node graph](/docs/node-graph).
- **Strand Mesh node.** A live tube or ribbon mesh of the strands, drawn in the viewport in place of
  the strands and following animation, Follow Scalp and Simulate. **Mesh in Scene** connects a Maya
  mesh to it for animated Alembic export. **Utilities > Strands to Mesh** stays a one-off snapshot.
- **Charcoal theme.** A calm, monochrome theme, and a theme picker: **GroomLab > Theme** in Maya,
  **View > Theme** in the app (on the next start).
- **App fix.** The cards preview showed every card with another card's texture; fixed.

Grooms saved before: nothing changes.

## 0.11.0 (October 16, 2026)

- **GroomLab is open source.** Apache-2.0, on GitHub at
  [thekesslereffect/groomlab](https://github.com/thekesslereffect/groomlab) (the Blender add-on is
  GPL-3.0-or-later). Still free for any use; donations on Ko-fi keep it going.
- **Import XGen grooms.** **GroomLab > Import > From XGen...** converts XGen Interactive Grooming
  and XGen Core descriptions into GroomLab grooms: guides, density, length, width, clump, noise,
  coil and cut, expressions, and Ptex or file textures (copied into the groom's masks folder). An
  import report lists what was mapped, approximated or skipped. See [Importing](/docs/importing).
- **Import Yeti grooms, without Yeti.** **GroomLab > Import > From Yeti...** reads Yeti's graphs and
  groom strands straight from the scene: scatter, grow, guides, clumping, scraggle, width, displace,
  cut and textures. A Roots field resolves texture paths that use environment variables.
- **Guides from reference photos (experimental).** **GroomLab > Guides > Guides from Reference...**
  opens a two-view window, the photo beside the mesh: pair a few landmarks to line the photo up
  (the camera solves live), draw flow arrows on the photo, touch up the hair mask, then
  **Generate** grows guides that follow the photo, with symmetry. **Measured Look** (Maya) reads
  clumps, curls, flyaways, frizz, tips and thin areas from the photo and offers to set them. Works
  best on long hair and longer fur. See [Reference photos](/docs/reference-photos).
- **The same strands on every machine.** GroomLab's math now gives bit-identical results on
  Windows and Linux, so a groom renders the same on any farm node.
- **Linux builds from source.** The core, the C API, the command line, the Python module and the
  Blender helper build and pass their tests on Linux (see the repository's docs/DEVELOPING.md).

Grooms saved before: strands differ in the last digits (the new portable math); re-bake caches if
you compare them bit for bit. In Python, `apply_suggested_look` is now `apply_measured_look`.

## 0.10.0 (October 14, 2026)

- **HairColor, simpler and fixed.** Hue Random and Saturation Random now vary every strand, in
  both modes, in the viewport, the app, Arnold and RenderMan. **Clump Share** gives each clump its
  own color (with a Clump before the HairColor; the node says so when there is none). HairColor
  follows RenderMan's PxrHairColor: Base (mode, melanin, redness, dye or color, map, greys),
  Random, Tip and Highlight. Every renderer uses the one RGB HairColor writes; in mode melanin it
  is the color whose absorption is the melanin's, so Arnold renders it as its melanin. Color
  params show a swatch in the Graph Editor (click it to pick); the Attribute Editor's Hair Color
  section shows every everyday param and the resulting color. See
  [Color patterns](/docs/color-patterns#haircolor).

  Grooms saved before: `rednessRandom`, `colorSource`, `dyeAmount` and `mapMode` are gone and
  ignored (a map now replaces the color in mode color and dyes the pigment in mode melanin);
  `valueRandom` works in mode color only (in mode melanin, raise `melaninRandom`). A mode melanin
  groom renders in Arnold by its RGB instead of the melanin user data, about as before. Exports
  that store a plain color (USD displayColor, Alembic Cd, Unreal's groom_color, the cards' Color
  maps) and Maya's hair tube shading now get the hair's appearance, measured from Arnold renders:
  natural colors come out somewhat darker and more saturated than before (medium brown sRGB about
  145, 108, 66), and picked colors lighter than the value picked, as the hair renders. See
  [Base color and appearance](/docs/rendering#base-color-and-appearance).
- **Color maps drive colors.** The Map node has an **rgb** output: link it to a color param
  (HairColor's color, dye or tip color, Bands and FeatherColor colors) and a painted or imported
  color map colors the hair. See [Painting maps](/docs/painting-maps).
- **Import an image as a map.** **Import...** in the Paint tab makes a texture map from an image
  in one step, named after the file, color or gray as the image is (`groomlab.import_map`).
- **One way to paint a setting.** The Width and Density brushes are gone. In the Paint tab,
  **+ New Map > For** a setting (Density, Width, Length, Clump strength, Curl, Noise amplitude,
  Flyaways, or any other) makes the map, links it and starts the Paint brush, in one undo step;
  a setting that already reads a map picks it. The painting brushes live in the Paint tab only.
  Ctrl on a gray map paints toward 0 (on a density map: removes hair); the Graph Editor's Paint
  starts at 1 like the rest.
- **Lighter defaults.** The viewport shows every strand by default (Preview Density 1), Scatter
  makes 80 strands per unit area (was 200) and the presets' densities are lower (hair 80, fur 25),
  so a new groom previews in full and stays responsive. Raise density for final renders.
- **Arnold IPR safety.** GroomLab now stops Arnold's render before it edits the hair during IPR
  (a newer MtoA could update it mid-render and crash Maya), never hands Arnold a negative width or
  a broken strand, and warns once when IPR runs on a groom of 500,000 strands or more. The crash
  log names the module that faulted and the GroomLab, Maya, MtoA and Arnold versions.
- **GPU fix.** Very dense grooms (past about 4 million strands) could get a few points wrong from
  the GPU preview's clearance pass; fixed.

Grooms saved before (besides HairColor above): scripts using the `width` or `density` brushes get
an error naming the new way; maps they made keep working. Scatter nodes with no density set now
make 80 strands per unit area instead of 200.

## 0.9.0 (October 13, 2026)

- **Pick a hair color.** HairColor has a **Mode**: Melanin (natural, as before) or **Color (RGB)**,
  which renders the picked color with no pigment on top, in the viewport, the app, Arnold,
  RenderMan and Blender. A tip color now applies as soon as you pick one, takes the strand's
  randomness, and **Tip Offset Random** varies where each strand's tip color starts. The groom's
  Attribute Editor has a new **Hair Color** section, and the app's Look card a Natural / Picked
  color switch. New presets: **white_wool** and the **two_tone_tips** add-on. See
  [Color patterns](/docs/color-patterns).
- **Arnold renders the groom's own colors.** A groom with no shader renders its look (it used to
  fall back to a fixed brown), and a shader made with Create Hair Shader is rebuilt when the look
  changes. Your own shaders are left alone.
- **RGB colors match Arnold in the viewport.** Picked colors, FeatherColor, Bands and color maps
  used to draw too dark and too saturated; the viewport and the app now turn the color into the
  fiber's absorption as aiStandardHair does.
- **Springy fur in Simulate.** A **Preset** menu on the Simulate node (Default, **Fur**), and
  **groomedUnderGravity**, which treats the groomed shape as already resting under gravity: fur
  holds its groom and still moves with the body. Stiff guides now hold their shape and come to
  rest, hair resting on the scalp or a collider keeps its length, and guides saved on an older
  version of the scalp follow a moving body when the groom has a rest mesh. See

- **Brushes.** A red line marks the symmetry plane on the scalp while a brush with symmetry is

  Width brush now always paints toward 0 (it painted toward the inverse of Value). Erase removes
  guides, parts and flow arrows; Density with Ctrl removes hair, which the tool now says.
- **Small screens.** The Graph Editor opens docked under the viewport when a floating window would
  crowd the screen (1080p at 125-150%, or 1366x768), the Transfer and Updates windows open within
  the screen, and the app's panels scroll when short.
- **Scatter.** A note on the node and a Script Editor warning past 2 million strands; `length`
  says when guides set it instead.

Grooms saved before: HairColor's map setting `mode` (replace or multiply) is now `mapMode`; a groom
that set it shows an error until it is changed. RGB-colored looks are lighter in the viewport (as in
Arnold). Grooms without a shader render their own colors in Arnold. Simulations come out
differently; bake again.

Simulations and their bakes come out slightly different; bake again.

## 0.4.0 (October 12, 2026)

- **Hair that doesn't pass through itself.** The new **SelfCollide** node pushes hair apart wherever
  it is packed tighter than real hair can be, so layers rest on one another and volume builds up
  behind the ears and at the crown. Hair with room to spare stays put. Lower `packing` for more
  volume. See [Node graph](/docs/node-graph#other-main-nodes).
- **Static.** The new **Static** node charges the hair: the outer layer puffs out, tips lift away
  from the head and clumped locks fray into single strands, like dry winter hair.
- **Parts found for you.** Set a Part node's `partsFrom` to `auto` and it finds the parts where you
  combed the hair apart, no drawing needed. `splitAngle` sets how sharp a split must be.
- **Attributes made clear.** Params that name an attribute now say whether they **Write** or
  **Read** it. Reading ones list only the attributes that actually reach the node, with the node
  each comes from; a node that would overwrite an earlier node's attribute warns you, and one that
  reads a name nothing writes gets the grey **i** badge. See
  [Node graph](/docs/node-graph#attributes).
- **All on the GPU.** Dragging the new nodes' params, and brushing guides before them, run on the
  GPU in Maya and the app.

## 0.3.0 (October 11, 2026)

- **Wet hair in one node.** The new **Wet** node clumps hair the way water does: small clumps near
  the root merge into bigger ones toward the tip, every small clump inside one big clump. One
  slider, `wetness`, sets how far it goes (animate it to dry the hair); `clumpSize` and `levels`
  set the clumps' sizes. It replaces stacking Clump nodes by hand, and the **wet** add-on preset
  now uses it. See [Node graph](/docs/node-graph#other-main-nodes).
- **Wet hair looks wet.** Hair shades darker, more saturated and glossier where it is wet, in the
  viewport, the app and Arnold, with nothing to set: from the Wet node, a Simulate with rain or
  water, or an Attribute named `wetness` (to wet hair by a map without clumping it).
- **Curls that hang under their weight.** Curl's new `weight` lets curls stretch where hair
  hangs below them: loose near the root, tight at the tip, narrowing as they stretch, like real
  ringlets. Hair lying flat or pointing up keeps its curls; `weightDirection` says which way is
  down. 0 by default, so existing grooms look the same.
- **Both brush and drag on the GPU.** Dragging `wetness` or `weight`, and brushing guides under a
  Wet node or a weighted Curl, run on the GPU in Maya and the app.
- **Fewer nodes.** **Comb** is now part of **Bend**: with `along` `direction`, Bend lays the
  direction onto the scalp and never bends past it, so `angle` 180 combs every strand that way.
  **Straighten** is now part of **Smooth**, whose new `scale` (the size of the bends it removes,
  a share of the strand's length) replaces `iterations`: small values take out kinks, 1
  straightens. Smooth now looks the same at any point count. The Comb, Smooth and Straighten
  *brushes* are unchanged.
- **Light hair matches Arnold.** Blonde tips were about a quarter brighter in the viewport and the
  app than in Arnold; they now match.
- **Several scalps, fixed.** Each scalp now subdivides at its own level, hair on a moving extra
  scalp jiggles with it, and scripted brush strokes reach every scalp.
- **Blend takes curls.** Blend no longer stops with "a and b must be the same strands" when a Curl
  on one side added points to tight curls (`blend_two_looks` on small scalps).

Grooms saved before: Comb and Straighten nodes no longer load. Rebuild a Comb as a Bend with
`along` `direction` (`angle` 180 for a full comb) and a Straighten as a Smooth with `scale` 1.
Smooth's `iterations` is gone: set `scale` (the default, 0.09, looks like the old default).

## October 9, 2026

- **GroomLab's own hair shading in the viewport.** Strands in Maya's viewport are lit with
  Marschner's three highlights from their pigment and RenderData's specular and roughness,
